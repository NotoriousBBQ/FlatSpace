- **Flat-output planetary improvements** — every current improvement (`Food1Production`, etc.) is a yield multiplier on top of a per-worker production rate, so it does nothing for a planet whose base/per-worker rate for that resource is 0 (e.g. Desolate's food: `_baseFoodProduction: 0`, `_foodProduction: 0` — no amount of improvement tier or worker allocation gives it any food). - **Periodic Distribution Center re-evaluation** — a DC is sticky by design (picked once, kept until lost — see `SelectDistributionCenter`), which is right for stability but means the coverage-maximizing candidate chosen early (e.g. turn ~100 on a 4-player 100-planet board) is never reconsidered as the empire keeps growing around it for the rest of a long match. A tuning-log review of a 400-turn run found Prime 0 (a turn-99 Food DC pick) losing its usual role as a producer to a different, better-positioned planet around turn 277 — not a bug (the shipment matrix correctly reallocates surplus toward worse shortages elsewhere), but a sign that "best DC candidate" can genuinely drift over a long match. Worth exploring a periodic (not reactive) re-scoring of existing DCs against the current colonized-planet layout, replacing one only if a candidate scores meaningfully better than the incumbent, so stability is preserved for the common case.

- **Weight planet value by connectivity** — *Consumer (1) assault and conversion target choice, and chokepoint bunker weighting, moved to completed_features on 2026-10-22. Still open (producer value by planet type moved to completed_features on 2026-10-22): garrison and defending-hub priority beyond the existing category 4, DC selection, the shipment threshold, and the stronger assault tilt (a chokepoint outranking a target with some committed ships; not built, see completed_features).* Use how connected a planet is (and, better, how many shortest routes pass through it) as a general planet-value signal, instead of judging planets only by cost, type or connection count. Evidence from three `4p.json` tuning runs (2026-09-30): each run had one lasting blockade occupation that took 60 to 90% of all blockade events, and they fell on planets that matter for different reasons. `Industrial 4` (5 connections) ranks 4th of 100 by shortest-path betweenness: it is the crossroads on most routes between its neighbours. `Normal 9` (6 connections) ranks 14th and sits next to Prime 2's home. `Verdant 4` ranks only 78th (108 pair-routes) but is a very specialised food producer, so it is valuable as a *producer*, not a chokepoint. So there are two distinct value kinds (chokepoint and specialised producer), and raw connection count alone misses the first (Verdant 1 has 8 connections and the top score; `Industrial 16` has 4 and ranks 2nd). `tools/planet-centrality.ps1` computes the ranking for any board config (`-Board`, `-Highlight`, `-Top`). Candidate consumers, roughly in order of payoff: (1) fleet target choice for assault and blockade (the Military Targets and Blockade Targets below; `AssaultPlanner` currently picks the cheapest known enemy planet, which tends to land on hubs near a rival's home only by accident); (2) garrison priority (`ShipTransportPlanner` category 4, "high traffic", uses a plain neighbour count today); (3) defending and re-taking the player's own hubs; (4) colonization target value; (5) Distribution Center selection; (6) the per-target resource shipment delivery threshold ("Value-aware shipment delivery threshold", below). **Raise this when tuning assault, blockade or garrison targeting.**

- **Value-aware shipment delivery threshold** — resource shipments through unavoidable blockades are refused when they would deliver less than `GameAIConstants.shipmentMinDeliveredFraction` (default 0.5) of what the origin pays; every target shares that one number today. *The seam exists: `PlayerAI.MinDeliveredFractionFor(targetPlanetName)`.* Make it depend on how valuable the target is, so high value planets accept lossier shipments (a lower fraction: even 10% of a shipment can keep a critical planet alive) and ordinary planets insist on a better return (a higher fraction). Candidate value signals, from cheapest: (1) Distribution Center status (`FoodDistributionCenters`/`GrotsitsDistributionCenters`; a DC feeds its whole neighbourhood); (2) specialised producers (Verdant for food, Desolate for grotsits, Farm, Industrial), which live on shipments by design (`_grotsitsImportAllowance`); (3) shortest-path betweenness from `tools/planet-centrality.ps1` (chokepoints; see "Weight planet value by connectivity" above); (4) how badly the planet is short (a planet about to lose population or go dead should accept almost any delivery). Evidence for tuning: `ShipmentLossy|<origin>-><target>|<amount>|<loss>` lines give the delivered share per shipment (`(amount - loss) / amount`) per target, and `ShipmentCancelled|<target>|LowYield` lines show which targets the fraction is refusing; a target that keeps logging `LowYield` while its population falls is a candidate for a lower fraction. Also weigh that a lossy shipment still pays its full amount at the origin, so a low fraction on many targets starves the source. Related: the Distribution Centers entry above, and the blockade avoidance unit.

- **Industry production centers and shippable industry** — two related ideas, not started (recorded 2026-09-30). *Today:* industry is strictly local. Each planet's worker industry (rate 7 on Industrial and Desert, 2 to 5 elsewhere, plus a base of 1 to 3) accumulates in `Planet.Industry` and is spent only on that planet's own production item, and `PlayerAI.BuildIndustryMatrix` picks what each planet builds with no regard to how much industry it has. Food and Grotsits can be shipped from a surplus planet to a shortage planet (`ResourceMatrix`, the shipment order trio, the route planner and blockade awareness); Industry cannot. `GameAI.ExecuteOrder` already handles `OrderTypeIndustryTransport` and `OrderTypeIndustryChange` (they add to the target planet's `Industry`), but nothing in `PlayerAI` emits them and there is no in-progress flag or matrix. (1) **Production centers:** develop the planets with the best industry yield (Industrial, Desert, high-tier industry improvements) as the player's production centers: bias expensive builds (warships, Update Warship, colony ships, the high tier improvements) toward them in `BuildIndustryMatrix`, and treat them as high value planets for garrison priority, assault and blockade targeting (a third value kind beside chokepoint and specialised producer in "Weight planet value by connectivity" above). (2) **Ship industry as a resource:** send surplus industry from high-industry planets to planets that cannot build fast enough themselves (new colonies, low-yield planets with a long queue, blockaded planets that need warships, see `blockadedWarshipBoost`), modelled like Food and Grotsits: a surplus-to-shortage matrix, a delayed transport plus an origin deduction plus an in-progress flag, `RoutePlanner.PlanShipmentRoute` and the blockade loss rules, and the delivered-fraction threshold (`MinDeliveredFractionFor`, see "Value-aware shipment delivery threshold" above). *Open questions:* what counts as surplus when a planet spends industry on its own queue every turn (a reserve for its current item); how shipped industry interacts with the roulette production choice (which planet spends it, and on what); whether industry should ship only toward items it can complete, since partial progress may be wasted; and that concentrating production makes a few hubs decisive, so blockades on them matter more. The two ideas complement each other: production centers produce the surplus that shipping distributes. Related: the Distribution Centers entry (a production center could double as a DC) and the blockade avoidance unit.

- **Industrial planets have no grotsits workers (worker allocation)** — analysis from `4p.json` tuning runs on 2026-09-30, not started. *Symptom:* P3 (a 5-connection hub home with three Farms, an Industrial planet and Verdant 3 around it, and no Desolate within 2 hops) repeatedly falls into a grotsits shortage, then a morale drop, then fewer colony ships; across 12 `4p.json` runs the weak P3 starts had 4.5 planets short of grotsits at T100 against 1.2 in the healthy ones. The `GrotsitsShort|<planet>|<Start or End>|<population>|<capacity>|<upkeep>|<morale>` lines show 0 of 909 P3 shortage episodes with demand above capacity, and `Industrial 11` was short for 324 turns in one run with about 3 times its demand in capacity. *Cause (from `Planet.AssignWorkForStrategy`, consistent with the logs but not yet seen at runtime):* the Industry strategies (`PlanetStrategyIndustry`, `FocusedIndustry`, which Industrial planets use by default, and Desert's) allocate Food, Industry, Research, then Grotsits, and each job asks for about population / rate workers, so on an Industrial planet (industry rate 7, research rate 3, food rate 3, grotsits rate 3, base grotsits 1) research absorbs every worker left after food and industry and grotsits gets 0. The planet then makes only its base grotsits against a demand of population + upkeep (modelled at population 10: 1 made against 11.5, so it imports 4 to 10 grotsits per turn; Industrial planets were 33 to 41% of P3's short planet-turns). This contradicts the design intent that specialised planets (Industrial included) be nearly self-funding, which `Planet.CanAffordImprovement` assumes by counting the full capacity. *Farm and Verdant planets are a different case:* their grotsits workers already come second (after food) but their grotsits rate is 1 and their base is 0, so a Farm at population 10 makes 8 against a demand of 10 and a Verdant at 15 makes 12 against 15: a structural 2 to 3 per turn that only imports can cover (and a new Farm or Verdant colony is short from its first turn). *Option A (recommended):* reorder the two Industry strategies to Food, Industry, Grotsits, Research. Industry output is unchanged (industry stays second and needs about 2 workers), Industrial planets self-fund from about population 7 (at 10: 13 made against 11.5), the cost is research on each Industrial planet (about 13 to 1 per turn at full population, so the player's research slows by an amount still to be measured), and no extra condition is needed because the grotsits requirement only asks for about demand / rate workers when stock is fine. Keep it strategy-agnostic: `Planet` does not know the owner's `AIStrategy`, and P3 switches to Consolidate at T24 to T28 in every run, before any shortage starts (T31+), so an Expand-only rule would never apply to it. *Option B:* set Industrial's initial strategy from 9 (`FocusedIndustry`) to 0 (`Balanced`), whose order is Food, Grotsits, Industry, Research (a data edit in `IndustrialPlanetTypeDataNoPop.asset`, but it affects every Industrial planet and loses the industry focus for leftover workers as yields rise); or switch a planet's strategy at runtime while it is short (more code for little extra gain: it needs hysteresis, and the Grotsits family puts grotsits before industry and sends all leftover workers to grotsits, which could starve industry while catching up; its one benefit is an Industrial planet exporting grotsits to adjacent Farms). Not recommended. *Optional second step:* the worker requirements ignore each planet's base production (`ResourceWorkerRequirement` divides by the rate only), so every planet assigns about one worker more than it needs (a Farm at population 10 asks for 3 food workers where 2 would do, taking a grotsits worker and adding about 1 to its deficit, roughly 3 to 4 grotsits per turn across P3's three Farms and Verdant planet); subtract the base from the food and grotsits requirements after Option A, and measure that separately. *Validation plan:* first a self-check that builds an Industrial planet at population 10 with stock 0 and asserts `GrotsitsWorkers == 0` (confirms the diagnosis with no extra logging), then swap the two blocks, then compare P3's short planet-turns, its morale and the `ResearchComplete` counts across three `4p.json` runs against the runs on file. Assumptions in the model: morale 100, no improvements (yield 1), low food stock; improvements raise yields over time, which shrinks the effect late. Desert planets put every worker on food (food rate 1), so they have no grotsits workers either, but they are a small share of P3's shortage. Related: `AssignWorkForStrategy` is an allowed tuning lever (see the Planet strategy notes in `CLAUDE.md`) and "Industry production centers and shippable industry" above.

- **Multiple combat rounds per turn** — ship combat resolves one round per turn today (`CombatSystem.Round`, start-of-turn snapshot, simultaneous, persistent damage). Run several rounds per turn instead, so a fight is decided faster and leftover force can carry on within the turn. *Not started; parked 2026-10-21 (owner's call) during planet defense brainstorming, which does not need it.* It would change every damage rate and tuning built on one round a turn (`combatDamageK`, `repairFractionPerTurn`, the loss-share window and surrender terms, `FightProjector`'s round count and `retreatProjectionTurns`), so retune after.

## Military Targets

- **Weight by connectivity** — see "Weight planet value by connectivity" above: rank enemy planets by shortest-path betweenness (chokepoints) and by specialised-producer value, not only by path cost.

- **High value planets colonized by another player** — weight assault targeting toward an enemy's most valuable colonies, not just any known enemy-held planet.
- **DCs controlled by another player** — an enemy Distribution Center is a natural high-priority target: taking or disrupting it undermines their whole supply network for that resource, not just one planet's output.
- **Planets colonized by another player blocking access to player colonized high value planets or DC placement** — an enemy-held planet that sits on the only (or cheapest) route to a player's own valuable colony or a good DC candidate site is a target worth prioritizing even if it isn't valuable itself, since holding it is what's actually constraining the player. Ties back to the tuning-log finding that a meaningful share of shipments already route through foreign-owned territory (5.12% of all shipments on `test2.json`, 3.56% on `4p.json`, in the last runs analyzed) — those same chokepoint planets are candidate targets.
