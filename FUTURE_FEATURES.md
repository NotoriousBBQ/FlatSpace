# Future Features

- **Human player mode** — today every player is AI-driven and the game only runs the simulation forward. Add a mode where a human controls a player and issues orders (shipments, colonization, production, research) instead of `PlayerAI`. See [`player_control.md`](player_control.md) for a broader exploratory discussion of externally-controlled players (human or LLM-driven), including what the underlying interface would take to build.
- **AIStrategyConsolidate** — a strategy phase the AI enters after first contact with another player, focused on fleet build, strategic colonization, and planet defense. *First cycle done: the first-contact trigger and Expand → Consolidate switch (Consolidate plays like Expand for now). Remaining: fleet build, strategic colonization, planet defense.* *Second cycle done: outer-only garrisons and assault targeting under Consolidate. Third cycle done: Consolidate's Warship production weight scales with the fleet shortfall. Remaining: strategic colonization, planet defense.*
- **Distribution centers** — today resource shipping moves the maximum amount of resources. In consolidate mode, change the model so the AI picks strategically located planets to serve as warehouses for resources.
- **Ship to ship combat** — warships engaging each other.
- **Blockade planet with docked ships** — ships docked at a planet can blockade it. *Done: docked offense blockades colony ships and food/grotsits shipments; see CLAUDE.md "Warships and Blockade".*
- **AI avoids blockaded routes and uses blockades** — the AI still launches colony ships and shipments into blockaded routes (wasted), and never moves warships in order to blockade (see Blockade Targets below).
- **Planetary invasion** — taking over another player's planet.
- **Board designer strategy disconnect** — the designer's per-planet `strategy` field (and `PlanetTypeDefaults.StrategyFor`, "Generate Names And Strategies", the map generator's `Strategy`) never reaches the running game: `PlanetSpawnData` carries no strategy and `Planet.Init` takes `CurrentStrategy` from the planet type's resource-data asset (`_initialStrategy`). Carry the designer's strategy through the board save into `PlanetSpawnData` and `Planet.Init`, or drop the field. Note the defaults already disagree (`StrategyFor(Prime)` is Balanced, Prime's resource data says Growth).
- **Flat-output planetary improvements** — every current improvement (`Food1Production`, etc.) is a yield multiplier on top of a per-worker production rate, so it does nothing for a planet whose base/per-worker rate for that resource is 0 (e.g. Desolate's food: `_baseFoodProduction: 0`, `_foodProduction: 0` — no amount of improvement tier or worker allocation gives it any food). A different improvement type that grants a flat, set resource output instead of a multiplier (e.g. a `PlanetBaseFoodImprovement1` that grants +3 food production outright) could give otherwise-zero-production planet types a real, tunable lifeline without changing their base type data.
- **Periodic Distribution Center re-evaluation** — a DC is sticky by design (picked once, kept until lost — see `SelectDistributionCenter`), which is right for stability but means the coverage-maximizing candidate chosen early (e.g. turn ~100 on a 4-player 100-planet board) is never reconsidered as the empire keeps growing around it for the rest of a long match. A tuning-log review of a 400-turn run found Prime 0 (a turn-99 Food DC pick) losing its usual role as a producer to a different, better-positioned planet around turn 277 — not a bug (the shipment matrix correctly reallocates surplus toward worse shortages elsewhere), but a sign that "best DC candidate" can genuinely drift over a long match. Worth exploring a periodic (not reactive) re-scoring of existing DCs against the current colonized-planet layout, replacing one only if a candidate scores meaningfully better than the incumbent, so stability is preserved for the common case.

## Military Targets

- **High value planets colonized by another player** — weight assault targeting toward an enemy's most valuable colonies, not just any known enemy-held planet.
- **DCs controlled by another player** — an enemy Distribution Center is a natural high-priority target: taking or disrupting it undermines their whole supply network for that resource, not just one planet's output.
- **Planets colonized by another player blocking access to player colonized high value planets or DC placement** — an enemy-held planet that sits on the only (or cheapest) route to a player's own valuable colony or a good DC candidate site is a target worth prioritizing even if it isn't valuable itself, since holding it is what's actually constraining the player. Ties back to the tuning-log finding that a meaningful share of shipments already route through foreign-owned territory (5.12% of all shipments on `test2.json`, 3.56% on `4p.json`, in the last runs analyzed) — those same chokepoint planets are candidate targets.

## Blockade Targets

- **Uncolonized high value planets** — deny a valuable but not-yet-colonized planet to the other players by blockading it before anyone can land a colony ship there.
- **DCs controlled by another player** — blockading an enemy DC denies it the shipments that keep its stockpile above target, without needing to take the planet outright.
- **Planets colonized by player through which other players' shipments pass** — the flip side of the Military Targets chokepoint idea: a planet the player already owns that sits on another player's shipping route is a candidate to actively blockade that route, not just pass through it. The same pass-through analysis used for Military Targets (shortest path between an enemy's origin/target, checking for the player's own territory as the intermediate) identifies these directly.
