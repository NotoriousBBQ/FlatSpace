# Future Features

- **Human player mode** — today every player is AI-driven and the game only runs the simulation forward. Add a mode where a human controls a player and issues orders (shipments, colonization, production, research) instead of `PlayerAI`. See [`player_control.md`](player_control.md) for a broader exploratory discussion of externally-controlled players (human or LLM-driven), including what the underlying interface would take to build.
- **AIStrategyConsolidate** — a strategy phase the AI enters after first contact with another player, focused on fleet build, strategic colonization, and planet defense. *First cycle done: the first-contact trigger and Expand → Consolidate switch (Consolidate plays like Expand for now). Remaining: fleet build, strategic colonization, planet defense.* *Second cycle done: outer-only garrisons and assault targeting under Consolidate. Third cycle done: Consolidate's Warship production weight scales with the fleet shortfall. Remaining: strategic colonization, planet defense.*
- **Distribution centers** — today resource shipping moves the maximum amount of resources. In consolidate mode, change the model so the AI picks strategically located planets to serve as warehouses for resources.
- **Ship to ship combat** — warships engaging each other.
- **Blockade planet with docked ships** — ships docked at a planet can blockade it. *Done: docked offense blockades colony ships and food/grotsits shipments; see CLAUDE.md "Warships and Blockade".*
- **AI avoids blockaded routes and uses blockades** — *(1) Colonization done: blockaded planets build no colony ships and do not colonize; colonization routes avoid visible blockaded planets and cancel when none exists; blockaded planets get a tunable warship/upgrade boost (`blockadedWarshipBoost`); see CLAUDE.md "Warships and Blockade".* Remaining:
  - (2) **Resource shipping** — *done: shipments route around visible blockaded planets (the route may exceed the maximum range); with no clean route they ship only if the amount exceeds the total blockade values along the least-loss route, otherwise they are cancelled. A blockaded source or target is an ordinary node on the route (its value counts), and a shipment is now cut at a blockaded origin on its first turn; see CLAUDE.md "Warships and Blockade".*
  - (3) **Assault breaks blockades:** target visibly blockaded planets with offense-based force sizing (dock enough offense that the value is 0 or less), include research priority changes for warships, and optionally prioritize blockaded planets that cut a colonization or shipment in the last N turns (tunable, default 5); the per-player blockade memory (`BlockadeMemory`, `PlayerAI.RememberedBlockades`) already records which planets recently cut a player's orders (planet, value, turn). Also the AI never moves warships in order to blockade (see Blockade Targets below).
  - (4) **Revisit garrison and colonization targeting with connectivity** — two places where a planet's connectivity is ignored today; evidence and the ranking script are in "Weight planet value by connectivity" below.
    - *Garrison:* `ShipTransportPlanner` decides where garrisons go by category, and its category 4 ("high traffic") counts a planet's neighbours (`GetNeighbours(name).Count >= highTrafficConnectionCount`). A plain neighbour count misses chokepoints: on `4p.json` `Industrial 4` (5 connections) is the 4th most central planet of 100, while `Verdant 1` has 8 connections and `Industrial 16` has only 4 but ranks 2nd. Revisit the categories and garrison sizes using shortest-path betweenness (`tools/planet-centrality.ps1`) so chokepoints are garrisoned and can be defended against the lasting blockade occupations seen in the tuning runs. Under Consolidate only outer planets garrison today, so decide how connectivity interacts with that too.
    - *Colonization:* `ProcessColonizers` scores targets by path cost alone (nearest valid target first, with the route planner only avoiding blockades). Weighing a target's connectivity would let the AI prefer to settle crossroads that secure its own routes (and deny them to a rival), and specialised producers, over merely nearby planets; it would also make the planets a player holds harder to cut off. Decide how much to trade off against distance and against the colony's survival odds (Desolate planets etc.).
  - (5) **Colonization avoidance for orders already in flight** — the blockade memory only protects colonists launched after a player learns of a blockade; colonists already flying toward it are still cut. In the `4p.json` tuning runs P2 lost three colonists at `Farm 1` at T379, T380 and T382, all launched before the planet was learned at T362, and routes there are longer than on `test2.json` (detours reach 11 nodes), so more colonists are in flight at any moment. Decide what an in-flight colonist should do when its remaining route crosses a newly learned or seen blockade: reroute around it, hold, or return (the colonist and food rider are already paid for), and how that interacts with the route the order carries (`GameAIOrder.Route`) and with where the ship is along it (order progress).
  - **Milestone:** sub-projects (2) to (5) together form one "unit" (2 is done; 3 to 5 remain), the owner's term for a grouping of features that comprise a major completion milestone (a subjective designation). Reaching the milestone means finishing all four, even though (4) and (5) are less interdependent with the others than (1) to (3) were. The grouping says nothing about how the work is delivered; specs, plans, branches and merges are decided when each is started.
- **Planetary invasion** — taking over another player's planet.
- **Board designer strategy disconnect** — the designer's per-planet `strategy` field (and `PlanetTypeDefaults.StrategyFor`, "Generate Names And Strategies", the map generator's `Strategy`) never reaches the running game: `PlanetSpawnData` carries no strategy and `Planet.Init` takes `CurrentStrategy` from the planet type's resource-data asset (`_initialStrategy`). Carry the designer's strategy through the board save into `PlanetSpawnData` and `Planet.Init`, or drop the field. Note the defaults already disagree (`StrategyFor(Prime)` is Balanced, Prime's resource data says Growth).
- **Flat-output planetary improvements** — every current improvement (`Food1Production`, etc.) is a yield multiplier on top of a per-worker production rate, so it does nothing for a planet whose base/per-worker rate for that resource is 0 (e.g. Desolate's food: `_baseFoodProduction: 0`, `_foodProduction: 0` — no amount of improvement tier or worker allocation gives it any food). A different improvement type that grants a flat, set resource output instead of a multiplier (e.g. a `PlanetBaseFoodImprovement1` that grants +3 food production outright) could give otherwise-zero-production planet types a real, tunable lifeline without changing their base type data.
- **Periodic Distribution Center re-evaluation** — a DC is sticky by design (picked once, kept until lost — see `SelectDistributionCenter`), which is right for stability but means the coverage-maximizing candidate chosen early (e.g. turn ~100 on a 4-player 100-planet board) is never reconsidered as the empire keeps growing around it for the rest of a long match. A tuning-log review of a 400-turn run found Prime 0 (a turn-99 Food DC pick) losing its usual role as a producer to a different, better-positioned planet around turn 277 — not a bug (the shipment matrix correctly reallocates surplus toward worse shortages elsewhere), but a sign that "best DC candidate" can genuinely drift over a long match. Worth exploring a periodic (not reactive) re-scoring of existing DCs against the current colonized-planet layout, replacing one only if a candidate scores meaningfully better than the incumbent, so stability is preserved for the common case.
- **Architecture overview for new engineers** — write a developer-facing overview of the game's architecture that a new engineer can read to get a grasp of the whole system, with class diagrams. `CLAUDE.md` documents each subsystem in depth but is written as agent guidance, not as an introduction. It should cover the simulation/presentation split (`GameAI` / `GameAIMap` / `Planet` / `Player` / `PlayerAI` on the runtime-created components, versus `Gameboard` and the UI Toolkit HUD); the turn loop and order pipeline (`GameAIOrder` and its types); decision-making through `ScoreMatrix`; pathing and the fog/knowledge/blockade visibility layers; ships, catalogs and research; saves; the Editor self-check pattern; and the tuning log. Include class diagrams (e.g. Mermaid, so they render on GitHub and stay in the repo) for the core object model and for the turn/order flow, plus a short "where do I change X" map.
- **Weight planet value by connectivity** — use how connected a planet is (and, better, how many shortest routes pass through it) as a general planet-value signal, instead of judging planets only by cost, type or connection count. Evidence from three `4p.json` tuning runs (2026-09-30): each run had one lasting blockade occupation that took 60 to 90% of all blockade events, and they fell on planets that matter for different reasons. `Industrial 4` (5 connections) ranks 4th of 100 by shortest-path betweenness: it is the crossroads on most routes between its neighbours. `Normal 9` (6 connections) ranks 14th and sits next to Prime 2's home. `Verdant 4` ranks only 78th (108 pair-routes) but is a very specialised food producer, so it is valuable as a *producer*, not a chokepoint. So there are two distinct value kinds (chokepoint and specialised producer), and raw connection count alone misses the first (Verdant 1 has 8 connections and the top score; `Industrial 16` has 4 and ranks 2nd). `tools/planet-centrality.ps1` computes the ranking for any board config (`-Board`, `-Highlight`, `-Top`). Candidate consumers, roughly in order of payoff: (1) fleet target choice for assault and blockade (the Military Targets and Blockade Targets below; `AssaultPlanner` currently picks the cheapest known enemy planet, which tends to land on hubs near a rival's home only by accident); (2) garrison priority (`ShipTransportPlanner` category 4, "high traffic", uses a plain neighbour count today); (3) defending and re-taking the player's own hubs; (4) colonization target value; (5) Distribution Center selection; (6) the per-target resource shipment delivery threshold ("Value-aware shipment delivery threshold", below). **Raise this when tuning assault, blockade or garrison targeting.**

- **Value-aware shipment delivery threshold** — resource shipments through unavoidable blockades are refused when they would deliver less than `GameAIConstants.shipmentMinDeliveredFraction` (default 0.5) of what the origin pays; every target shares that one number today. *The seam exists: `PlayerAI.MinDeliveredFractionFor(targetPlanetName)`.* Make it depend on how valuable the target is, so high value planets accept lossier shipments (a lower fraction: even 10% of a shipment can keep a critical planet alive) and ordinary planets insist on a better return (a higher fraction). Candidate value signals, from cheapest: (1) Distribution Center status (`FoodDistributionCenters`/`GrotsitsDistributionCenters`; a DC feeds its whole neighbourhood); (2) specialised producers (Verdant for food, Desolate for grotsits, Farm, Industrial), which live on shipments by design (`_grotsitsImportAllowance`); (3) shortest-path betweenness from `tools/planet-centrality.ps1` (chokepoints; see "Weight planet value by connectivity" above); (4) how badly the planet is short (a planet about to lose population or go dead should accept almost any delivery). Evidence for tuning: `ShipmentLossy|<origin>-><target>|<amount>|<loss>` lines give the delivered share per shipment (`(amount - loss) / amount`) per target, and `ShipmentCancelled|<target>|LowYield` lines show which targets the fraction is refusing; a target that keeps logging `LowYield` while its population falls is a candidate for a lower fraction. Also weigh that a lossy shipment still pays its full amount at the origin, so a low fraction on many targets starves the source. Related: the Distribution Centers entry above, and the blockade avoidance unit.

- **Industry production centers and shippable industry** — two related ideas, not started (recorded 2026-09-30). *Today:* industry is strictly local. Each planet's worker industry (rate 7 on Industrial and Desert, 2 to 5 elsewhere, plus a base of 1 to 3) accumulates in `Planet.Industry` and is spent only on that planet's own production item, and `PlayerAI.BuildIndustryMatrix` picks what each planet builds with no regard to how much industry it has. Food and Grotsits can be shipped from a surplus planet to a shortage planet (`ResourceMatrix`, the shipment order trio, the route planner and blockade awareness); Industry cannot. `GameAI.ExecuteOrder` already handles `OrderTypeIndustryTransport` and `OrderTypeIndustryChange` (they add to the target planet's `Industry`), but nothing in `PlayerAI` emits them and there is no in-progress flag or matrix. (1) **Production centers:** develop the planets with the best industry yield (Industrial, Desert, high-tier industry improvements) as the player's production centers: bias expensive builds (warships, Update Warship, colony ships, the high tier improvements) toward them in `BuildIndustryMatrix`, and treat them as high value planets for garrison priority, assault and blockade targeting (a third value kind beside chokepoint and specialised producer in "Weight planet value by connectivity" above). (2) **Ship industry as a resource:** send surplus industry from high-industry planets to planets that cannot build fast enough themselves (new colonies, low-yield planets with a long queue, blockaded planets that need warships, see `blockadedWarshipBoost`), modelled like Food and Grotsits: a surplus-to-shortage matrix, a delayed transport plus an origin deduction plus an in-progress flag, `RoutePlanner.PlanShipmentRoute` and the blockade loss rules, and the delivered-fraction threshold (`MinDeliveredFractionFor`, see "Value-aware shipment delivery threshold" above). *Open questions:* what counts as surplus when a planet spends industry on its own queue every turn (a reserve for its current item); how shipped industry interacts with the roulette production choice (which planet spends it, and on what); whether industry should ship only toward items it can complete, since partial progress may be wasted; and that concentrating production makes a few hubs decisive, so blockades on them matter more. The two ideas complement each other: production centers produce the surplus that shipping distributes. Related: the Distribution Centers entry (a production center could double as a DC) and the blockade avoidance unit.

## Military Targets

- **Weight by connectivity** — see "Weight planet value by connectivity" above: rank enemy planets by shortest-path betweenness (chokepoints) and by specialised-producer value, not only by path cost.

- **High value planets colonized by another player** — weight assault targeting toward an enemy's most valuable colonies, not just any known enemy-held planet.
- **DCs controlled by another player** — an enemy Distribution Center is a natural high-priority target: taking or disrupting it undermines their whole supply network for that resource, not just one planet's output.
- **Planets colonized by another player blocking access to player colonized high value planets or DC placement** — an enemy-held planet that sits on the only (or cheapest) route to a player's own valuable colony or a good DC candidate site is a target worth prioritizing even if it isn't valuable itself, since holding it is what's actually constraining the player. Ties back to the tuning-log finding that a meaningful share of shipments already route through foreign-owned territory (5.12% of all shipments on `test2.json`, 3.56% on `4p.json`, in the last runs analyzed) — those same chokepoint planets are candidate targets.

## Blockade Targets

- **Weight by connectivity** — see "Weight planet value by connectivity" above. The lasting occupations seen in the `4p.json` tuning runs (`Industrial 4`, `Normal 9`, `Verdant 4`) were a crossroads, a hub and a food specialist, so a target scorer should weigh both chokepoint value and producer value.

- **Uncolonized high value planets** — deny a valuable but not-yet-colonized planet to the other players by blockading it before anyone can land a colony ship there.
- **DCs controlled by another player** — blockading an enemy DC denies it the shipments that keep its stockpile above target, without needing to take the planet outright.
- **Planets colonized by player through which other players' shipments pass** — the flip side of the Military Targets chokepoint idea: a planet the player already owns that sits on another player's shipping route is a candidate to actively blockade that route, not just pass through it. The same pass-through analysis used for Military Targets (shortest path between an enemy's origin/target, checking for the player's own territory as the intermediate) identifies these directly.
